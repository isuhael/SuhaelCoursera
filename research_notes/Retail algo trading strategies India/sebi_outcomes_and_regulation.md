## Q1. SEBI's F&O profitability studies: Jan 2023 (FY22), Sep 2024 (FY22–FY24), Jul 2025 (FY25), Aug 2026 (FY25–FY26)

### Takeaway
Every SEBI study since 2023 finds that roughly 9 in 10 individual equity-F&O traders lose money: 89% in FY22, 91.1% in FY24, about 91% in FY25 and 87.7% in FY26. Individuals' aggregate net losses peaked at about ₹1.05–1.12 lakh crore in FY25 and fell to ₹91,685 crore in FY26, mainly because fewer people traded. Over the same years, proprietary traders and FPIs made large gross profits, and 96–99% of those profits came from algorithmic trading.

- Age, FY26: under-30s are the largest cohort, and their loss rate was **91.9%**. — [X article by CA Ghansham Joshi summarising the study](https://x.com/CaGhanshamJoshi/article/2090787106801189107); [Riddhi Siddhi](https://www.riddhisiddhisharebrokers.com/sebi-fo-traders-loss-study-fy26)
- Capital: about **90% of traders using less than ₹1 lakh lost money, against about 81% of those using more than ₹1 lakh** (one summary only). — [Futuresense India](https://futuresenseindia.com/news/individual-traders-equity-derivatives-losses-sebi-study/)

### Inferences
- The lower FY26 loss total comes mostly from **fewer participants** (about −20%), not better outcomes per trader. The average loss per trader rose from about ₹1.1 lakh to ₹1.17 lakh, and the loss rate is still close to 88%.
- Before costs, about 82% lose. After costs, about 88% lose. Costs of about ₹25,000 crore a year (about ₹26,000 per trader in FY24) turn a meaningful minority of small gross winners into net losers. For a retail bot, **costs per trade (STT, brokerage, exchange fees, slippage) are a first-order concern**.
- Algo-using prop firms and FPIs keep taking 96–99% of institutional F&O profits. A retail algo is competing against colocated, institution-grade algos on the other side of the trade.

### Gaps
- I could not open the SEBI PDFs, so the following are not extracted: income-band breakdowns, city-tier breakdowns, the FY24 cost split between brokerage, exchange fees and STT, and the exact unique-trader counts per study.
- The Sep 2024 PR number (37/2024 versus 22/2024) is unresolved.
- FY25 is reported as ₹1,05,603 crore (July 2025 study), about ₹1.06 lakh crore (government reply) and about ₹1.12 lakh crore (Aug 2026 study). The difference is most likely sample coverage, but this is not confirmed.
- No SEBI F&O study beyond the August 2026 one was found as of 29 Oct 2026.

## Q2. SEBI's intraday equity-cash study (FY23)

### Takeaway
The intraday study was **released on 24 July 2024, not July 2023**, and covers FY23. It found that **71% of individual intraday traders in the cash segment lost money**. The share of loss-makers rose to **80% among traders making more than 500 trades a year**. Loss-makers paid a further **57% of their trading losses** in trading costs.

### Cited Findings
- **71% (7 in 10)** of individual intraday traders in equity cash made a net loss in FY23. The average loss was **about ₹5,371**. — [Business Standard, 24 Jul 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/over-70-intra-day-traders-incur-losses-during-fy23-reveals-sebi-study-124072401110_1.html); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/7-in-10-intraday-traders-in-equity-cash-suffered-losses-in-fy23-sebi-study-124072400975_1.html)
- Frequency: the share of loss-makers **rose to 80% for very frequent traders (more than 500 trades a year)**. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/over-70-intra-day-traders-incur-losses-during-fy23-reveals-sebi-study-124072401110_1.html); [Motilal Oswal](https://www.motilaloswal.com/learning-centre/2024/7/sebi-study-reveals-key-insights-on-individual-intraday-traders-in-india)
- Size: **76% of traders with annual intraday turnover above ₹1 crore lost money**, with an average loss of **₹34,977**. Do not confuse this with the overall ₹5,371. — [Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/70-pc-of-investors-burnt-money-in-intraday-trading)
- Costs: **loss-makers paid a further 57% of their trading losses in trading costs**, and **profit-makers paid 19% of their trading profits** in trading costs. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/7-in-10-intraday-traders-in-equity-cash-suffered-losses-in-fy23-sebi-study-124072400975_1.html); [Morung Express](https://morungexpress.com/sebi-study-warns-against-intra-day-trading-risks-in-equity-cash-as-young-traders-rise)
- Age: **76% of traders under 30 lost money**. Under-30s made up **48% of intraday traders in FY23, up from 18% in FY19**. Intraday participation rose **more than 300%** compared with FY19. — [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/market-updates/sebi-highlights-intraday-trading-risk); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/7-in-10-intraday-traders-in-equity-cash-suffered-losses-in-fy23-sebi-study-124072400975_1.html)
- Method: the sample was individual clients of the top-10 brokers, about 86% of individual clients in the segment. A working group of academics, brokers and market experts peer-reviewed the study. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/over-70-intra-day-traders-incur-losses-during-fy23-reveals-sebi-study-124072401110_1.html)

### Inferences
- Losses per trader in intraday cash are much smaller than in F&O: about ₹5k versus about ₹1.1 lakh. Loss rates still rise with trading frequency, which bears directly on any high-turnover intraday bot.
- Costs equal to 57% of trading losses mean costs make losses worse by more than half. Strategies with thin per-trade edge are unlikely to survive costs.

### Gaps
- No later SEBI intraday-cash study (FY24 onwards) was found.
- The exact split of trading costs (brokerage, STT, exchange charges) in the intraday study was not extracted.

## Q3. How many traders make real profits, and option buyers versus sellers

### Takeaway
Only **about 1% of individual F&O traders earned more than ₹1 lakh in profit over FY22–FY24 after costs**. Only about 7.2% made any profit over those three years. In FY26, loss rates were highest for option **buyers**: about 90% of "only-buyers" lost. Sellers lose more often per rupee lost but are far fewer.

### Cited Findings
- **Only about 1% of individual traders earned profits above ₹1 lakh** after transaction costs (Sep 2024 study, FY22–FY24). — [Wright Research](https://wrightresearch.in/blog/sebi-futures-and-option-report-individual-traders-in-fandos-incur-rs-18-lakh-crore-loss-over-3-years); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/markets/fo-frenzy-over-93-retail-investors-lost-average-rs-2-lakh-in-three-years-says-sebi)
- **Only about 7.2% of individual F&O traders made a profit** over the three years FY22–FY24 (one outlet). — search extract of [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/93-of-individual-traders-suffered-losses-in-fo-in-last-3-years-sebi-study)
- FY22: 11% were profitable, with an average profit of ₹1.5 lakh. — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/markets/stocks/story/loss-makers-of-dalal-street-9-out-of-10-individual-fo-traders-lost-money-in-fy22-says-sebi-367628-2023-01-25)
- FY26, buyers versus sellers: **about 90% of "only option buyers" were loss-makers**. A "mostly option buyers" cohort is reported at **about 75%** (single analysis). Option sellers who lost money lost much more per person, but few sellers lost. About **97% of traders are predominantly option buyers** and about **2% predominantly option sellers**. — [Finnovate](https://www.finnovate.in/learn/blog/sebi-fno-trader-losses-fy26-individual-derivatives); [Replete Equities](https://www.repleteequities.com/blog/sebi-latest-report-what-the-data-reveals-about-options); [Upsides](https://upsides.in/blog/why-9-in-10-fo-traders-lose-money-what-sebis-fy26-study-means-for-beginners/)
- FY26: **92% of individuals' aggregate losses came from options**. — [EdgeX](https://pro.edgex.exchange/en-US/news/article/sebi-study-fo-traders-lost-money-fy26)

### Inferences
- If SEBI's distributions are representative, a small retail trader's base rate of earning more than ₹1 lakh net over three years is about 1 in 100. Any backtest showing steady F&O profits should be checked hard against costs and slippage.
- Nearly all retail traders are option buyers, and buyers have the highest loss rate. Selling, whether directly or via spreads, carries large tail losses per loser and needs much more margin.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the exact FY26 buyer/seller loss percentages, or the definitions of "only" and "mostly" buyers, against the SEBI PDF. The 75% figure is single-source.
- No buyer/seller split was found for the 2023 and 2024 studies.
- No SEBI figure was found for the share of traders earning more than ₹1 lakh in FY25 or FY26.

## Q4. SEBI's index-derivative measures (October 2024) and later changes (2025–2026): what is in force now

### Takeaway
The 1 Oct 2024 circular, phased in between 20 Nov 2024 and 1 Apr 2025, is still the base framework. The current overlays are:
- Weekly expiries only on Tuesday or Thursday: Nifty 50 weekly on **Tuesday** since 1 Sep 2025, Sensex weekly on **Thursday**.
- New lot sizes since the January 2026 expiries: **Nifty 65, Bank Nifty 30**.
- FutEq (delta-based) open interest and position limits, from the May 2025 circular.
- A pre-open session for F&O, from December 2025.
- **Higher STT from 1 Apr 2026**: futures 0.05%, options premium 0.15%.

SEBI's chair said in early 2026 that no further curbs were planned. Only one weekly benchmark per exchange remains. Measured impact so far: unique F&O traders down about 20%, and premium turnover down about 9–11%.

## Q5. The retail algo framework (SEBI circular of 4 Feb 2025) and what Zerodha's Kite Connect requires now

### Takeaway
SEBI's 4 Feb 2025 circular, with NSE's implementation standards of 5 May 2025, is **fully applicable from 1 April 2026**, after deferrals from 1 Aug 2025 and then 1 Oct 2025, plus a glide path for brokers. The main requirements:
- Open APIs are banned. Access needs a broker-issued API key, OAuth with 2FA, and **a static IP whitelisted by the broker**.
- Algos above **10 orders per second (OPS) per exchange per client** must be registered with the exchange and tagged with an Algo-ID.
- **Black-box** (undisclosed-logic) algo providers must be SEBI-registered Research Analysts. White-box providers are only empanelled with exchanges.

On Zerodha, a self-coded personal bot needs a static IP from 1 Apr 2026, is capped at 10 OPS, and does **not** need registration below 10 OPS. Kite Connect costs ₹500 a month for market and historical data. The order-only Personal tier is free.

### Cited Findings
**The SEBI circular and its timeline**

**What the framework requires**
- **Broker as principal:** retail investors reach approved algos only through registered brokers. The broker needs exchange permission for each algo. Algo providers are not SEBI-regulated directly but must be **empanelled with exchanges**, and are treated as the broker's agents. Brokers do due diligence on providers and handle investor complaints. — [SEBI circular page](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/feb-2025/safer-participation-of-retail-investors-in-algorithmic-trading_91614.html); [IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/civil/sebi-retail-investors-algorithmic-trading/); [K&S Partners](https://ksandk.com/corporate/sebis-new-regulatory-framework-for-retail-algorithmic-trading/)
- **White box versus black box:** white-box algos (logic disclosed and replicable, such as execution algos like VWAP, TWAP and slicers) versus black-box algos (logic undisclosed). **Black-box providers must register as SEBI Research Analysts** and keep a research report for each algo. Any change in a black box's logic must be registered as a new algo. — [IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/civil/sebi-retail-investors-algorithmic-trading/); [QuantInsti](https://blog.quantinsti.com/sebi-algo-trading-guidelines-retail-investors)
- **Self-built algos by tech-savvy retail traders:** these must be registered through the broker **only above an OPS threshold**, and may be used only by the trader and **family** (self, spouse, dependent children, dependent parents). — [SEBI circular page](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/feb-2025/safer-participation-of-retail-investors-in-algorithmic-trading_91614.html); [IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/civil/sebi-retail-investors-algorithmic-trading/)
- **Threshold:** NSE's implementation standards (5 May 2025) set it at **10 orders per second per exchange per client**. Below 10 OPS, no registration is needed; above it, registration is mandatory. — [IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/civil/sebi-retail-investors-algorithmic-trading/); [Zerodha Z-Connect, overview of NSE circular](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/general/a-comprehensive-overview-of-nses-circular-on-the-new-retail-algo-trading-framework)
- **API security:** open APIs are prohibited. Access needs a unique vendor- and client-specific API key, a **static IP whitelisted by the broker**, mandatory **OAuth**, and **2FA**. A client may register **1–2 static IPs**, and IP sharing is allowed only within family, with 2FA-verified consent. — [IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/civil/sebi-retail-investors-algorithmic-trading/)
- **Traceability and control:** every algo order carries an **exchange-assigned unique Algo-ID**. Exchanges keep a **kill switch** for each Algo-ID. — [SEBI circular page](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/feb-2025/safer-participation-of-retail-investors-in-algorithmic-trading_91614.html); [IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/civil/sebi-retail-investors-algorithmic-trading/)
- Some blogs say retail algo servers must be hosted in India. Not confirmed from a primary source. — [Sahi](https://www.sahi.com/blogs/sebi-algo-trading-rules-2026-what-every-retail-trader-must-know-before-april)

## Q6. How a small trader's F&O and intraday profits are taxed in India

### Takeaway
F&O on a recognised exchange is **non-speculative business income**. Intraday equity (no delivery) is **speculative business income**. Both are taxed at **slab rates** and reported on **ITR-3**. F&O losses can offset non-salary income and carry forward 8 years. Speculative losses offset only speculative income and carry forward 4 years. From 1 Apr 2026 the Income-tax Act 2025 replaces the 1961 Act; F&O's non-speculative status reportedly moves from the proviso to s.43(5) to s.66. Audit thresholds depend on turnover, computed by the absolute-profit method, and on the share of digital transactions.

### Cited Findings
- F&O on a recognised exchange is non-speculative under the proviso to s.43(5) of the IT Act 1961. Intraday equity is speculative. Delivery trades are generally capital gains. Business income is taxed at slab rates. — [Groww (AY 2026-27)](https://groww.in/blog/how-traders-are-taxed-in-india); [Sahi](https://www.sahi.com/blogs/income-tax-on-f-and-o-trading-in-india-complete-guide-for-fy-2025-26); [Quicko](https://learn.quicko.com/income-tax-trading)
- **Income-tax Act 2025:** s.43(5) of the 1961 Act corresponds to **s.66 of the 2025 Act, from 1 Apr 2026** (per tax blogs). — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1056974); [Kanakkupillai](https://www.kanakkupillai.com/learn/fo-trading-income-tax/)
- Losses: F&O (non-speculative) losses can offset any head except salary in the same year. Unabsorbed losses **carry forward 8 assessment years**, against business income only. Speculative (intraday) losses offset only speculative income and carry forward **4 years**. Carry-forward requires filing the return on time. — [Groww](https://groww.in/blog/how-traders-are-taxed-in-india); [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/fo-trader-return-filing)
- Return form: ITR-3, or ITR-4 under presumptive taxation. **Turnover is not contract value.** It is computed by the absolute-profit method. — [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/fo-trader-return-filing); [1 Finance](https://1finance.co.in/1f-dashboard/?p=7918)
- Tax audit under s.44AB:
  - The turnover limit is **₹10 crore if at least 95% of receipts and payments are digital**, otherwise ₹1 crore. Above the limit, audit is compulsory whatever the profit or loss.
  - A trader eligible for s.44AD (presumptive 6% of digital turnover, ceiling ₹2–3 crore depending on source) who declares a loss or less than 6%, and whose income is above the basic exemption, may need an audit under s.44AB(e) read with s.44AD(4). Sources disagree on this.
  — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/tax-audit-requirement-f-o-transactions.html); [Tax2Win](https://tax2win.in/guide/future-and-options-income-business-income); [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/fo-trader-return-filing)

### Inferences
- A small trader who loses money in F&O should still file ITR-3 on time to keep the 8-year carry-forward. If they declare a loss while under the s.44AD limits, they should check with a CA whether s.44AB(e) applies.
- Mixing intraday cash trading into a bot makes part of the income speculative, with weaker loss-offset rules. Keeping a bot to F&O, or to delivery trades, simplifies the tax treatment.

### Gaps
- The new section numbers under the Income-tax Act 2025 for carry-forward and audit (equivalents of old s.72, s.73, s.44AB and s.44AD), and the return due dates for FY2026-27, were not confirmed from the statute. One source gives 31 Aug 2026 as the deadline for carry-forward, which looks inconsistent with usual due dates.
- Not covered: whether STT is deductible as a business expense, and how the April 2026 STT increase affects that.
- All tax sources are blogs or guides from tax-filing platforms, not CBDT text.
