# Shrink the Target Before Building the Bot

No strategy on record makes ₹2,000 a day from ₹1 lakh, and chasing that number is the surest way to lose the account. ₹2,000 a day is **2% a day**. That works out to about **42% a month**, ₹5 lakh a year on a fixed ₹1 lakh, or **141 times your money in a year** if profits are reinvested. That is roughly 13 times the reported after-fee return of Renaissance's Medallion fund, the best trading record known. Standard bet-sizing maths says it needs a return-to-risk ratio (Sharpe ratio, explained below) above 3, held every year after all costs. Betting bigger to hit it turns even a real edge into probable ruin. "5–7% a month" is a different and much smaller target: ₹5,000–7,000 a month, or **₹238–333 a day**. It is not ruled out by arithmetic, but it still compounds to **80–125% a year**, about double Medallion's reported net. No group of Indian or overseas retail traders has been shown to sustain it. The base rates are harsh. **87.7% of individual F&O traders lost money in FY26, with an average loss of about ₹1.17 lakh**, which is more than this whole account. The profits went to algorithmic proprietary desks (firms trading their own money) and foreign funds. At ₹1 lakh, four things shut most doors: Zerodha's charges after April 2026, index contracts worth about ₹15 lakh each, SEBI's margin rules, and fixed data and static-IP fees. Futures, unhedged option selling, arbitrage and market-neutral books are all out of reach, and intraday option trading pays 3–8% of capital a month in charges before it earns a rupee. One approach is affordable, cheap to run, reasonably supported by evidence and able to cope with a falling Nifty: a slow bot that rotates between Nifty, gold and cash ETFs using trend rules. Run that live and keep everything else on paper until the account grows. A good outcome is **15–25% a year, or ₹60–100 per trading day**. Earning ₹2,000 a day at that rate needs about ₹20–33 lakh of capital, so the honest route to the goal runs through savings and a proven live record, not leverage.

## ₹2,000 a day demands a 141-fold year that nobody has produced

The two targets are very different numbers. ₹2,000 a day on ₹1,00,000 is **2% per trading day**. Over a 21-day trading month that is ₹42,000, or **42% a month** (51.6% if profits are reinvested). Over about 250 trading days it is **₹5 lakh a year, or 500%, on a fixed ₹1 lakh**. Reinvested, 1.02^250 ≈ **141, so ₹1 lakh would become about ₹1.41 crore in twelve months**. "5–7% a month" is ₹5,000–7,000 a month, or **₹238–333 a day**: 60–84% a year if profits are withdrawn, 80–125% if they are reinvested. The ₹2,000 goal is six to eight times the 5–7% goal. Put the other way round, earning ₹2,000 a day at 5–7% a month would need **₹6–8.4 lakh of capital**, and earning it at a still-excellent 15–25% a year would need **₹20–33 lakh**.

Whether a target is reachable depends on risk as much as on return. The usual yardstick is the **Sharpe ratio**: average return above a safe rate, divided by how much returns swing. Above 1 is good; a sustained, after-cost Sharpe above 3 is normally seen only in high-frequency and market-making operations, which cannot be scaled up indefinitely. The **Kelly criterion** is the formula for the bet size that grows capital fastest without going broke. It ties the two together: the best possible long-run growth rate is about half the Sharpe ratio squared. Compounding at 2% a day therefore needs a **Sharpe of at least 3.16 even when betting the full Kelly amount, and 3.65 at the safer half-Kelly**. That has to hold every year, after every cost. Even at full Kelly the account has **about a 50% chance of halving at some point** ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/kelly-criterion/)), and a **1-in-3 chance of halving before it doubles**, against 1-in-9 at half Kelly ([Albion Research](https://www.albionresearch.com/kelly/)). Now grant an elite Sharpe of 3. A 2% daily average then comes with **daily swings of about 10.5%**, and if returns were normally distributed, one day in twenty would lose about 15% of the account. The Nifty's annual volatility is about 16.5% ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/personal-finance/low-volatility-index-funds-etfs-show-lower-drawdowns-and-higher-returns-125040300960_1.html)), roughly 1% a day. The target therefore needs about ten times the index's risk, which is what an account fully invested in options, or holding one Nifty futures lot at about 15 times leverage, would carry.

A simulation run for this research shows what happens when a real edge meets a fixed daily target. Take an idealised strategy that wins 55% of even-money bets, makes one trade a day, has a known edge and pays no costs. Its Kelly bet is 10% of capital. To average 2% a day it must stake 20% per trade, twice Kelly. At that size, expected compound growth turns negative even though every single trade has a positive edge:

| Stake per trade | Kelly multiple | Median wealth after 250 trades | Chance of a ≥50% fall from peak | Chance of ending below half | Chance of a losing year |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.5% | ¼ Kelly | 1.77× | 1% | 0.1% | 8% |
| 5% | ½ Kelly | 2.69× | 35% | 2% | 13% |
| 10% | Full Kelly | 3.16× | 97% | 10% | 23% |
| 20% (the "2% a day" stake) | 2× Kelly | 0.79× | 100% | 40% | 51% |

At 20% stakes a handful of lucky paths push the *average* result to about 185 times, which is how "2% a day" screenshots circulate while most accounts using the same approach shrink. The daily target fixes the bet size, and that bet size is a ruin strategy.

Translated into option trades, the hurdle is concrete. Say the bot makes three Nifty option round trips a day (buy and sell), each costing about ₹135 including assumed slippage. It must then earn about **₹2,430 gross a day, or ₹810 per trade on average after the losers**. Risking 20 Nifty points (₹1,300 per lot) to make 40 needs a **win rate of at least 54%, every day**. Risking 10 points to make 20 needs 75%. The best-documented rule-based intraday option-buying test in India wins about **48%** of the time ([In The Money by Zerodha](https://inthemoneybyzerodha.substack.com/p/how-to-trade-opening-range-breakout)). Losing streaks then do the damage. With a 45% loss rate over 250 trades, there is about a **20% chance of at least one run of eight straight losses** and about a 69% chance of a run of six. Ten straight losses cost 9.6% of the account at 1% risk per trade, but 40% at 5% risk and 65% at 10%. Recovery is lopsided: after a 50% loss you need a 100% gain to get back to even.

The 5–7% target is not ruled out by arithmetic, but it is not a monthly plan either. Compounding 5–7% a month at half Kelly needs a sustained after-cost Sharpe of about 1.25–1.5. The account would swing 30–84% a year, depending on how good the edge really is, which is two to five times Nifty's risk. Costs make it harder. One Nifty option round trip a day plus the ₹500 data fee costs about ₹3,342 a month, so the bot must make 8.3–10.3% gross to keep 5–7%. At two trades a day, costs of about ₹6,183 a month exceed the entire net target. Returns also never arrive in neat monthly instalments. Even strategies with strong long-run records go a full year with nothing: the two big Nifty200 Momentum 30 index funds returned 0.53–0.66% over the year to August 2026 ([arthgyaan](https://arthgyaan.com/fund/kotak-nifty-200-momentum-30-index-fund-direct-growth/151781); [arthgyaan](https://arthgyaan.com/fund/motilal-oswal-nifty-200-momentum-30-index-fund-direct-growth/149800)). For context, top-quartile Indian portfolio managers (PMS) are cited at about 18–25% a year ([Aequitas](https://www.aequitasindia.in/more/pms-returns-india/)). This research's estimate is that a skilled retail algo trader who survives might aim for **15–40% a year, with falls of 15–30% along the way**. Even that would put them in the top few percent of participants. A realistic plan for ₹1 lakh therefore aims for **₹60–100 per trading day in a good year**, and has to allow for losing years.

## Nine in ten lose, and charges decide the rest

### The money flows from individuals to algorithmic desks

SEBI has published four studies of individual F&O traders, and the loss rate has never dropped below about 88%. **89%** lost in FY22 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/markets/sebi-study-suggests-89-retail-traders-in-equity-f-o-suffered-losses-123012501466_1.html)). Over FY22–FY24, **93% of more than one crore traders lost money, an average of about ₹2 lakh each**, and **only about 1% made more than ₹1 lakh after costs** across the three years ([SEBI](https://www.sebi.gov.in/media-and-notifications/press-releases/sep-2024/updated-sebi-study-reveals-93-of-individual-traders-incurred-losses-in-equity-fando-between-fy22-and-fy24-aggregate-losses-exceed-1-8-lakh-crores-over-three-years_86906.html); [Wright Research](https://wrightresearch.in/blog/sebi-futures-and-option-report-individual-traders-in-fandos-incur-rs-18-lakh-crore-loss-over-3-years)). In FY25 about **91%** lost, and net losses rose 41% to **₹1,05,603 crore** ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/net-losses-of-traders-in-fo-widens-in-fy25-sebi-study-125070701221_1.html)). In FY26, **87.7%** lost and total losses fell to **₹91,685 crore**. But the **average loss rose to about ₹1.17 lakh**, and **92% of losses came from options** ([People Plus](https://www.peopleplus.press/en/business/stock-market/details/sebi-study-877-percent-of-individual-fo-traders-lost-money-in-fy26-as-options-drove-most-losses); [Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/retail-fo-traders-lose-91685-crore-in-fy2526-despite-sebi-curbs-average-loss-hits-117-lakh-govt/81318.html)). The FY26 improvement came mostly from about 18–20% fewer people trading, not from better results per trader ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/active-derivatives-traders-fall-18-in-fy26-to-8-75-million-sebi-study-126082001378_1.html)). One caveat: these figures come from news and broker coverage of the SEBI studies, because the SEBI PDFs could not be opened during this research.

Costs alone turn a meaningful slice of winners into losers. About 82% of individuals lost before costs in FY26, against 87.7% after; the before-cost figure comes from a single blog ([Finnovate](https://www.finnovate.in/learn/blog/sebi-fno-trader-losses-fy26-individual-derivatives)). About **4.4 lakh traders who were profitable before costs ended the year in loss** after individuals together paid **₹24,859 crore in transaction costs** ([Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/sebi-futures-options-study-transaction-costs-traders-losses-fy26)). Secondary coverage reports three more splits. About **90% of traders who only bought options lost money** ([Finnovate](https://www.finnovate.in/learn/blog/sebi-fno-trader-losses-fy26-individual-derivatives)). The loss rate climbs from about 91% in a trader's first year to **more than 95% among those trading four or more years in a row** ([Futuresense India](https://futuresenseindia.com/news/individual-traders-equity-derivatives-losses-sebi-study/)). And about 90% of traders using less than ₹1 lakh lost, against about 81% of those using more (same source, single summary). None of these splits was checked against the SEBI PDF, but they all point the same way: experience and persistence do not rescue small option traders.

On the other side sit the algorithmic desks. In FY26, proprietary traders made about **₹44,483 crore** in gross derivatives profit and foreign portfolio investors (FPIs) about ₹13,896 crore. **About 99% of those profits went to firms that trade by algorithm** ([BusinessToday](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/story/proprietary-traders-derivatives-profits-fall-in-fy26-says-sebi-study-550558-2026-08-21)). In FY24 the shares were 96% and 97% ([Wright Research](https://wrightresearch.in/blog/sebi-futures-and-option-report-individual-traders-in-fandos-incur-rs-18-lakh-crore-loss-over-3-years)). Servers placed inside the exchange itself ("co-location") were reported to handle 62.1% of equity-derivatives turnover in 2024, a secondary figure ([Investing.com India](https://in.investing.com/news/colocation-hits-357-algo-trading-surges-to-53-the-techdriven-shift-in-nse-4655643)).

The Jane Street case shows what these firms can extract. SEBI's July 2025 interim order alleged that four Jane Street entities made more than ₹36,500 crore from Indian index derivatives between January 2023 and March 2025. The alleged method was moving Bank Nifty stocks and futures on expiry days while holding much larger options positions. SEBI impounded about ₹4,843 crore ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/07/04/indian-regulator-bars-us-trading-firm-jane-street-from-accessing-securities-market.html); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/sebi-bars-jane-street-orders-rs-4844-cr-disgorgement-market-manipulation-125070400081_1.html)). The allegations are disputed and still before the appeals tribunal: in October 2026 SEBI said the order could be vacated if Jane Street shows its trading was not manipulative ([BusinessToday](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/story/sebi-tells-sat-that-jane-street-has-enough-data-to-respond-to-market-manipulation-charges-559953-2026-10-06)). The lesson for a retail bot is that being "algorithmic" is not itself an edge. The winners' edges are speed, quoting both sides of the market, huge balance sheets and the ability to trade stocks, futures and options together. A Kite Connect script has none of these.

Cash-market day trading and foreign data tell the same story. SEBI found that **71% of individual intraday equity traders lost money in FY23, rising to 80% among those making more than 500 trades a year**. Loss-makers paid a further 57% of their trading losses in costs ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/7-in-10-intraday-traders-in-equity-cash-suffered-losses-in-fy23-sebi-study-124072400975_1.html)). Taiwan's complete 1992–2006 trading record is the largest dataset available. In it, **less than 1% of day traders could predictably earn positive returns after fees**. The top 500 earned 0.38% a day after fees the following year, about a fifth of 2% ([Barber et al.](https://www.escholarship.org/uc/item/7k75v0qx)), and 74% of day-trading volume came from traders with a history of losses ([Barber et al. 2020](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/ouprasset/v_3a10_3ay_3a2020_3ai_3a1_3ap_3a61-93..htm)). In Brazil, **97% of people who day-traded index futures for 300 or more days lost money**, and only 1.1% earned more than the minimum wage. The single best trader averaged US$310 a day, with daily swings of about US$2,560. Performance did not improve with experience ([Chague et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101)).

### What a round trip costs on Zerodha after April 2026

Zerodha's pricing model has not changed. Delivery trades pay no brokerage, and intraday, futures and options pay ₹20 per executed order (or 0.03% if lower). What changed is tax. The Union Budget 2026-27 raised securities transaction tax (STT, a government levy on each trade) **on futures from 0.02% to 0.05% and on option premiums from 0.10% to 0.15%, from 1 April 2026** ([Zerodha Support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/account-opening/resident-individual/ri-charges/articles/how-is-the-securities-transaction-tax-stt-calculated); [5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/news/stt-hike-on-fo-to-take-effect-from-april-1-amid-rising-options-activity)). The table below adds those rates to Zerodha's displayed exchange, SEBI, stamp-duty and GST charges ([Zerodha charges](https://zerodha.com/charges/)). It shows the explicit cost of one round trip (buy and sell at the same price) and the monthly drag on a ₹1 lakh account over 21 trading days. Caveat: these rates come from search-engine extracts of Zerodha's pages, which could not be loaded directly, and two cached versions differ slightly. Re-check every figure on zerodha.com/charges and Zerodha's brokerage calculator before trading.

| Same, plus assumed 0.5-point slippage each way | ₹135 | 2.1 points | 5.7% / 8.5% / 14.2% |
| Nifty futures, 1 lot | ₹882 (STT is ₹738 of it) | 13.6 points | 37% / 56% / 93% |
| Intraday equity, ₹1 lakh | ₹83 | 0.08% | 3.5% / 5.2% / 8.7% |

| MCX Gold Mini, 1 lot | ₹302 | ₹30 per 10 g | 12.7% / 19.0% / 31.7% |

Three things stand out. First, STT now dominates futures: it is 84% of a Nifty futures round trip, and the April 2026 hike doubled that round trip from about ₹439 to ₹882. Second, options look cheap in rupees, but for a one-lot account brokerage plus GST is about two-thirds of the explicit cost. Zerodha charges ₹20 per order however many lots the order carries, so a five-lot backtest understates what a one-lot account pays per unit of exposure. Third, the slippage line, which is an assumption rather than measured data, roughly doubles the cost of options. At two to three trades a day the drag alone is 6–9% of capital a month, more than the whole 5–7% target.

Then come the fixed costs, which hurt small accounts most. Kite Connect's order and account APIs (the "Personal" tier) are free. Live and historical market data cost **₹500 a month**, cut from ₹2,000 ([kite.trade forum](https://kite.trade/forum/discussion/comment/49590); [OneTradeJournal](https://onetradejournal.com/trading-apis/kite-connect-personal-vs-connect)). Zerodha's own support article still says ₹2,000, and whether the ₹500 includes GST is unresolved ([Zerodha Support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/kite-api/articles/what-are-the-charges-for-kite-apis)). That is **₹6,000–7,080 a year, or 6–7% of a ₹1 lakh account**. A static IP address, now required for orders sent through the API, costs about ₹100–250 a month from third-party providers ([staticip.in](https://staticip.in/brokers/zerodha)). An intraday bot therefore starts each year **7–10% behind before it places a single trade**, which matches or beats a typical year of Nifty returns. Profits are also taxed at your income-tax slab rate (see the plan section), and the cost table ignores that.

### ₹15 lakh contracts lock a ₹1 lakh account out of most structures

SEBI's October 2024 framework set a minimum value of ₹15 lakh for each index-derivative contract ([SEBI](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/oct-2024/measures-to-strengthen-equity-index-derivatives-framework-for-increased-investor-protection-and-market-stability_87208.html)). Today's lot sizes reflect it: **Nifty 65 units, Bank Nifty 30, Sensex 20** ([Sahi](https://www.sahi.com/blogs/nifty-lot-size-2026-bank-nifty-sensex); [HDFC Sky](https://hdfcsky.com/news/nse-revises-market-lot-sizes-for-major-index-derivatives-effective-january-2026); [AlgoTest](https://algotest.in/blog/sensex-lot-size.md)). With the Nifty at 22,687.65 on 22 October 2026 (single source) ([JM Financial](https://www.jmfinancialservices.in/market-news-and-insights/1735600)), one Nifty lot controls about **₹14.75 lakh**. Third-party estimates put the margin for one Nifty futures lot at **₹1.1–1.5 lakh, more than the whole account** ([Sahi](https://www.sahi.com/blogs/nifty-futures-explained-lot-size-expiry-margin-and-strategy); [OneTradeJournal](https://onetradejournal.com/glossary/span-margin)). If you could hold one, a 1% Nifty move would be worth ₹14,755, or 14.8% of capital.

Selling options without a hedge needs similar margin. A short straddle (selling a call and a put at the same strike) on a Nifty option expiring that day ("0DTE", zero days to expiry) needs about **₹3–3.25 lakh** ([In The Money by Zerodha](https://inthemoneybyzerodha.substack.com/p/position-sizing-part-3-sizing-option)). An iron condor sells a call and a put and buys further-out options as insurance. One lot is typically quoted at **₹40,000–80,000 of margin**, with blog estimates ranging from ₹15,000 to ₹2 lakh ([GoPocket](https://www.gopocket.in/blog/iron-condor-strategy-india-nifty-secrets-2026); [NiftyTrader](https://www.niftytrader.in/markets/iron-condor-strategy-when-to-use-avoid/)). On expiry day an extra 2% "extreme loss margin" applies to short options ([Arihant Capital](https://blog.arihantcapital.com/sebi-tightens-the-reins-introduces-6-new-measures-for-fo-trading/)), which this report calculates at about ₹29,500 per short lot at today's Nifty level. Only buying options is cheap. A pricing model, not a live quote, puts an at-the-money weekly Nifty option at ₹100–200, or **₹6,500–13,000 per lot**.

Nifty weekly options expire on Tuesdays and Sensex weeklies on Thursdays. Bank Nifty and Fin Nifty now have only monthly contracts ([Zerodha Support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/trading-faqs/f-otrading/articles/weekly-expiries-discontinued-for-certain-index-derivatives); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/markets/stocks/story/nse-receives-sebi-nod-for-tuesday-expiry-bse-thursday-480736-2025-06-17)). On MCX, Gold Mini needs roughly ₹59,000–96,000 of margin and Silver Mini ₹42,000–69,000, so either would tie up half the account or more ([BhaavBrief](https://bhaavbrief.in/learn/mcx-lot-sizes); [Sahi](https://www.sahi.com/blogs/commodity-margin-lot-size-2026)). Crude Oil Mini (10 barrels, about ₹95,700 of crude at mid-October prices) fits, though its quoted ₹4,000–6,000 margin looks low and should be confirmed on Zerodha's margin calculator. The tiny Gold Petal and Silver Micro contracts fit easily, but Zerodha Varsity warns that liquidity outside the big gold contracts is "quite bad", with wide spreads ([Zerodha Varsity](https://zerodha.com/varsity/chapter/gold-part-1/)).

## The evidence favours slow, rule-based trading over fast trading

Across the strategy families, the pattern is consistent. The strategies retail traders crowd into, intraday option buying and expiry-day trades, have the worst after-cost evidence. The ones with real, documented sources of return (volatility selling, momentum, trend following, arbitrage) either need far more than ₹1 lakh or have crashed badly in live trading. The table summarises; the sections below give the detail and the sources.

|---|---|---|---|
| Intraday option buying (opening-range breakout) | Zerodha net-of-cost test, Jan 2022–Feb 2026: ~48% win rate, positive expectancy, fading since mid-2024 | Weakly positive, decaying | One lot fits, but its stop-loss is ~2.6% of capital and costs run 3–6% a month |
| Expiry-day "hero-zero" buying | Vendor test, Oct 2025–Sep 2026: 16% win rate, profit factor 0.51 | Negative | Fits; avoid |
| Intraday short straddle (9:20) | The premium is earned mostly overnight; a vendor variant lost ₹1.1 lakh after costs in 2025–26 | Negative recently | No: ~₹3 lakh margin unhedged |
| Positional short straddle/strangle | Zerodha 7-year test: 5 of 7 years profitable; 2025–26 vendor test: profit factor 0.81 | Mixed, prone to big tail losses | No: unhedged margin exceeds capital |
| Hedged iron condor / iron fly | Volatility premium documented; no post-2024 return data | Unknown | Barely: one lot uses 40–80% of capital |
| Index futures trend following | Trend rules work in sharp falls and fail in sideways markets | Mixed | No: ~₹1.1–1.5 lakh margin per lot |

| Short-term mean reversion | Overreaction documented; no Indian after-cost tests | Unknown | Yes, on daily prices |
| Intraday equity breakout / VWAP / gap | 71% of intraday traders lose; 80% of the most active | Negative for the population | Fits; dominated by costs |
| Pairs trading / statistical arbitrage | Futures pairs up to 34% a year (2011–2017, before the cost hikes) | Dated | No: each futures leg is worth lakhs |
| Cash-futures arbitrage | Arbitrage funds ~6.5% a year over 5 years | Small but positive | Do-it-yourself no; through a fund, yes |
| Long-short / market-neutral | Long-short SIFs −1.8% vs Nifty ~−14% in 2026 | Cuts losses; not truly neutral | No |

### Option buying: the crowd's favourite has the worst record

Option buying is where retail money goes and where most of it is lost. A study of NSE retail trades across the whole market finds that 75% of retail option premium goes into index options. Activity surges in the final days before expiry, until retail traders made up 42% of same-day-expiry volume. The study estimates retail losses of ₹506 billion, and finds that when costs rose, traders shifted into cheaper, further out-of-the-money options and did worse ([Agarwal et al., CFR working paper](https://www.cfr-cologne.de/download/workingpaper/cfr-25-09.pdf)).

The best-documented rule-based buying system is Zerodha's **opening-range breakout (ORB)** test on Nifty options. The rules: buy a strike priced near ₹200 when its premium breaks out of its 9:15–11:15 range, take at most one call and one put a day, and use a 20% stop-loss. From January 2022 to February 2026, net of brokerage, taxes and 0.2% slippage, it showed **positive expectancy with a win rate of about 48%**. But the equity curve got bumpy after January 2024, had not recovered from a drawdown that began in mid-2024, and 2025 was described as "rough" ([In The Money by Zerodha](https://inthemoneybyzerodha.substack.com/p/how-to-trade-opening-range-breakout)). A separate 30-minute ORB on the Nifty index itself, over 2,122 trades, won 48.7% of the time with a **profit factor** (gross wins divided by gross losses) of 1.23, costs not stated. Its edge came from winners averaging about twice the size of losers ([Intraday Lab](https://intradaylab.com/blog/nifty-orb-breakout-strategy-backtest)). Expiry-day "hero-zero" buying of options priced under ₹10 did far worse: 19 trades from October 2025 to September 2026, a 16% win rate, a profit factor of 0.51 and a loss after costs ([QuantsApp](https://www.quantsapp.com/backtesting/strategies/expiry-day-hero-zero-otm-buy)).

To use option buying at all, use systematic breakouts on liquid strikes near the current price, with hard stops, and skip lottery tickets. Even the best test depends on rare large winners, and its edge is fading. At ₹1 lakh the fit is poor. The tested 20% stop on a ₹200 strike is ₹2,600 per lot, or **2.6% of the account per trade**, more than double the 1% ceiling that sound sizing suggests. On top of that, costs at one or two trades a day eat 3–6% of capital a month, plus the ₹500 data fee.

### Option selling: a real premium with a fat tail

Selling options is the only option strategy with a documented structural edge. Peer-reviewed work finds that Indian index options carry a variance risk premium ([International Review of Economics & Finance](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1059056020301222)). In plain terms, the volatility priced into options tends to be higher than the volatility that actually follows, so sellers collect something like an insurance premium. A non-peer-reviewed comparison of India VIX with the Nifty volatility that followed finds the premium positive on about 80% of days, averaging about 2.5 volatility points. Its worst reading was −64.5 points at the start of COVID ([GitHub study](https://github.com/RajolKumar2003/volatility-risk-premium-india)). The key finding comes from a 2024 study in the *Journal of Futures Markets*: the premium is **mainly payment for holding risk overnight** ([Bhat 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/fut.22512?af=R)). An intraday 9:20 straddle, closed by 3:15 pm, never holds that risk.

The tail decides the outcome. The same market-wide study estimates a ₹11 billion retail loss during COVID that **wiped out all the gains retail option writers had ever made** ([Agarwal et al.](https://www.ualberta.ca/en/finance-department/media-library/documents/vikas-agarwal.pdf)). On 13 March 2020 the Nifty hit its 10% lower circuit within five minutes of opening, then swung to more than 3% up within two hours ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/markets/short-sellers-caught-off-guard-as-market-swings-within-hours-of-opening-120031301418_1.html)). That kind of whipsaw triggers the stops on both legs of a short straddle. On election-results day, 4 June 2024, the Nifty closed down 5.93%, and a 23,250 put expiring two days later jumped **from ₹171.45 to ₹1,362.50, about eight times, in a single session** ([NiftyScanner](https://niftyscanner.in/labs/option-premium/2024-06-04/); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/stock-market-live-updates-june-4-sensex-nifty-lok-sabha-election-results-2024-psu-stocks-nse-124060400081_1.html)).

Recent backtests are discouraging. Zerodha tested selling Nifty straddles and strangles about 45 days before expiry over seven years, with a 50% profit target and a stop at twice the premium collected. It was profitable in five of the seven years, and a VIX filter lifted strangle win rates to about 85% ([In The Money by Zerodha](https://inthemoneybyzerodha.substack.com/p/we-backtested-the-famous-45-dte-strategy)). But one-year vendor backtests for October 2025 to September 2026 were negative:

| Vendor backtest, Oct 2025–Sep 2026 | Result |
|---|---|
| 45-day monthly straddle | Profit factor 0.81; worst fall about ₹1.15 lakh ([QuantsApp](https://www.quantsapp.com/backtesting/strategies/monthly-short-straddle-45dte-50pt-21dte)) |
| Overnight straddle | +₹24,344 before costs; −₹3,372 after ([QuantsApp](https://www.quantsapp.com/backtesting/strategies/overnight-short-options-close-to-open)) |
| 9:20 straddle with stop-and-reverse | −₹1,09,914 after costs ([QuantsApp](https://www.quantsapp.com/backtesting/strategies/short-straddle-sl-flip-to-long-leg)) |

Practitioners also report that 9:20 straddle stop-losses get deliberately triggered ("hunted") in thin mid-day markets ([Marketcalls](https://www.marketcalls.in/futures-and-options/how-the-9-20-intraday-straddlers-are-being-gamed.html)). One useful finding came from a Sensex 0DTE straddle test. Splitting six lots into six staggered entries raised return per unit of worst-case fall from 0.64 to 2.56 ([In The Money by Zerodha](https://inthemoneybyzerodha.substack.com/p/sensex-0dte-straddle-or-strangle)). Staggering, though, needs several lots, which ₹1 lakh cannot fund. Treat any widely shared "optimised" straddle claiming 80% a year ([Medium](https://medium.com/@amit179.iitk2/optimized-920-straddle-strategy-to-get-more-than-80-return-annually-e977453dca80)) as curve-fitting. Tradetron, an algo platform, warns its own users that picking the best of 50 strategies on one year of data "can show an excellent Sharpe ratio, yet the same strategy on the next year returns to nothing" ([Tradetron](https://tradetron.tech/blog/why-did-my-algo-strategy-fail-common-mistakes-and-fixes)).

To use option selling well, hold positions for several days rather than intraday, so you collect the overnight premium. Always hedge with bought wings, and size so the worst case is survivable. Filter entries by volatility, stagger them, and stay out around scheduled events such as elections or budgets. None of that fits in ₹1 lakh. One hedged lot needs ₹40,000–80,000 of margin. With 200-point wings its maximum loss is about ₹13,000 per side, less the premium collected. And four legs mean eight ₹20 orders per round trip, or ₹160 of brokerage before GST, STT and slippage.

### Trend following: wins in crashes and strong trends, bleeds in sideways markets

Trend following buys what is rising and sells, or exits, what is falling, without trying to forecast. A study of 80 trend rules on the Nifty from 2005 to 2012 found they worked well in sharply falling markets and poorly, or not at all, in slowly rising, mixed or trendless ones ([Indian Journal of Finance](https://www.indianjournalofentrepreneurship.com/index.php/IJF/article/view/71980)). StockViz's backtests on Zerodha Varsity, at an assumed cost of 0.25% per trade, found three things ([Zerodha Varsity, Part II](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/varsity/could-trend-following-be-a-successful-trading-strategy-part-ii); [Part III](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/varsity/could-trend-following-be-a-successful-trading-strategy-part-iii)). An equal-weight Nifty-plus-gold moving-average system cut the worst fall to about 10%, against about 20% for either asset alone, with returns in the mid-teens. A four-asset MCX basket reached mid-20s returns with 2× leverage, but crude oil was "the biggest culprit" for underperformance. And "a flat market is kryptonite". Academic studies of momentum in MCX commodities find weak results after costs ([JCRM](https://jcrm.psgim.ac.in/index.php/jcrm/article/view/657)).

Trend following works best with several assets that do not move together, long lookback periods chosen before you see any results, and no dropping of losing assets after the fact. StockViz itself noted that removing crude and copper with hindsight would flatter the results. At ₹1 lakh, index futures are out of reach, and a single MCX contract is too coarse to size properly. One Crude Mini lot holds about ₹96,000 of crude, so a ₹500-a-barrel move against you costs ₹5,000, or 5% of the account.

### Equity momentum and factor tilts: a genuine premium that just crashed

Momentum means buying the stocks that have risen most over recent months. India has one of the strongest documented momentum premiums: IIM Ahmedabad's factor data show winners minus losers averaging **21.9% a year** (gross of costs, long winners and short losers) from 1993 to 2013 ([IIMA](https://www.iima.ac.in/publication/four-factor-model-indian-equities-market)). The Nifty200 Momentum 30 index shows an 18.8% a year total return since 2005, against 13.9% for the Nifty 200. But everything before its August 2020 launch is back-calculated rather than live ([HDFC MF](https://files.hdfcfund.com/s3fs-public/Others/2026-07/HDFC%20NIFTY200%20Momentum%2030%20Index%20Fund_PPT%20%28July%202026%29.pdf)). Its live test went badly. The index **fell about 31.8% from September 2024 to April 2025** ([Curious Investing Insights](https://curiousinvestinginsights.substack.com/p/momentum-or-mayhem-unpacking-the)), and a Kotak fund tracking it returned 0.66% over the year to August 2026, against a 7.24% category average, without regaining its previous peak ([arthgyaan](https://arthgyaan.com/fund/kotak-nifty-200-momentum-30-index-fund-direct-growth/151781)). Across 97 published stock-return patterns, returns fell by about 58% after the research was published ([McLean & Pontiff](https://www.gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf)). Low-volatility stocks were the more defensive tilt: by the fund house's own calculation they averaged −5.5% in years when the Nifty 100 fell, against −13.6% for the index ([HDFC MF](https://files.hdfcfund.com/s3fs-public/Others/2026-08/HDFC%20Nifty100%20Low%20Volatility%2030%20Index%20Fund%20-%20Leaflet%20%28as%20on%20July%202026%29.pdf)). Copying a 30-stock momentum basket with ₹1 lakh is clumsy and creates tax on every rebalance. An index fund does it more cheaply, so treat momentum as an investment rather than a bot.

### Mean reversion and intraday equity: plausible ideas without after-cost proof

Mean reversion bets that a sharp move will partly reverse. Indian prices do overreact: stocks reverse within minutes of extreme moves ([Tripathi & Aggarwal](https://publishingindia.com/archive/ijfm/prior-return-effect-in-indian-stock-market-an-intra-day-analysis)), and large, liquid stocks reverse for up to six months after big monthly moves ([Parthasarathy & Sendilvelu](https://ceeol.com/search/article-detail?id=1054639)). But this research found no India-specific backtest with after-cost results for RSI(2), Bollinger-band or other short-term reversal rules. Reversals that play out in minutes are the territory of co-located desks. Intraday breakout, VWAP and gap strategies on Indian stocks also have no credible independent after-cost evidence, and SEBI's numbers show the most active intraday traders lose most often. Dip-buying in an index ETF on daily prices, long only and with a trend filter, is cheap enough to test. But in 2026's falling market it would have sat mostly in cash.

### Arbitrage, pairs and long-short: real edges that need lakhs per leg

The cleanest Indian evidence on pairs trading comes from a 2011–2017 thesis. It found pairs built from stock futures **significantly profitable after costs, at up to 34% a year**, while pairs in the cash market were not ([Krishikosh](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/items/79c40268-bdee-41b3-8d31-c9998a994d25/full)). It predates the 2024 lot-size increases and the 2026 STT hike. Holding a short position overnight in India requires futures or securities lending, because cash-market shorts must be closed the same day ([Zerodha Z-Connect](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/trending/shorting-and-indian-capital-markets)). A pair therefore needs two futures legs, each worth several lakh.

Cash-futures arbitrage (buying a stock and selling its future to lock in the price gap) is reliable but thin. Arbitrage mutual funds averaged about 6.5% a year over five years, and between 4.1% and 8.1% in each calendar year from 2021 to 2025 ([arthgyaan](https://arthgyaan.com/funds/categories/arbitrage-hybrid-funds)). IIM Bangalore research calls STT "a kind of spoilsport" that often exceeds the price gap for small traders ([IIMB](https://repository.iimb.ac.in/handle/2074/18746)). A single do-it-yourself position needs roughly ₹7–7.5 lakh ([Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/podcast/cash-future-arbitrage); attribution unverified).

Long-short funds show what professionals achieve with shorting at scale. Equity long-short specialised investment funds (SIFs) were down 1.8% in 2026 up to mid-October, against a Nifty down about 14%, and took 59% of the fall in the first quarter of 2026 ([5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/index.php/blog/sif-performance-data-shows-wide-gap-across-long-short-strategies-in-2026)). That is smaller losses, not market-neutral profit, and it is out of reach for a ₹1 lakh account.

## Falling markets reward rotation, not prediction

The weakness you describe is real. The Nifty set no record high in 2025, after 65 of them in 2024 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/nifty-misses-new-highs-this-cy-after-record-2024-gold-takes-the-spotlight-125101300069_1.html)). By mid-October 2026, the Nifty was reported down 14.4% and the Sensex 15.6% for the year, amid about ₹3.08 trillion of net foreign selling ([Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/economy/fpi-selling-reaches-3-08-trillion-in-cy26-as-october-2026-outflows-cross-47-000-crore); single source). Meanwhile **gold rose 72% and silver 122% in 2025, against 7% for the Nifty 500** ([5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/index.php/news/gold-rallied-72-and-silver-122-in-2025-while-nifty-500-rose-7)). MCX gold hit a record of about ₹1,62,500 per 10 g in August 2026 and pulled back to about ₹1,50,177 by 30 September, yet was still up more than 28% over the year ([StartupTalky](https://startuptalky.com/news/gold-silver-prices-india-today-26-august-2026-both-ease-further/); [StartupTalky](https://startuptalky.com/news/gold-silver-prices-india-today-30-september-2026/); single aggregator).

No strategy wins whatever the market does. Each so-called direction-neutral approach has a kind of market it hates. Option sellers profit from quiet, range-bound markets and are hurt by sudden jumps like 4 June 2024. Option buyers need big moves and lose premium to time decay in quiet markets. Trend followers profit from sustained moves and bleed in sideways, choppy markets. Market-neutral books need the ability to short at scale, which ₹1 lakh cannot buy. A strategy can be neutral to direction without being neutral to conditions, so the practical goal is to hold assets or strategies that suffer in different conditions.

For a small account, the cheapest way to do that is to rotate among assets that do not move together. Gold has moved more against the Nifty than silver has ([Zerodha Varsity](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/varsity/could-trend-following-be-a-successful-trading-strategy-part-ii)). **Dual momentum** holds whichever of the Nifty and gold has the stronger recent return, and falls back to cash or fixed deposits when neither beats them. Indian backtests of it report:

| Dual-momentum backtest | Result |
|---|---|
| Nifty vs gold vs fixed deposit, 2014–2024 | 18.79% a year, against 12.13% for the Nifty and 10.86% for gold ([Samasthiti](https://samasthiti.substack.com/p/indian-edition-of-the-dual-momentum)) |
| Quarterly NIFTYBEES/GOLDBEES switch, 14 years | 16.06% a year; worst fall −23.51% (unverified forum backtest) ([TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/dual-momentum-strategy-with-niftybees-goldbees/174470)) |
| Capitalmind analysis | Nifty-like returns with lower volatility ([Capitalmind](https://premium.capitalmind.in/2022/06/should-gold-be-in-your-long-term-portfolio/)) |

The first two backtests differ by about 2.7 percentage points a year, a reminder that results depend on the lookback period chosen. The 2025–2026 market would plausibly have kept such a system in gold for much of the period, but that is an inference, not a tested result. Two warnings point the other way. The August–September 2026 pullback in metals showed how quickly leadership can turn. And the Nifty-to-gold ratio hit a five-year low in April 2026, a level that has historically come before equity rallies ([share.market](https://www.share.market/buzz/insights/the-nifty-to-gold-ratio-a-compass-for-investors-in-times-of-war-and-market-volatility/)). Rotation does not predict; it follows, and it pays for that with false signals when trends flip.

## A ₹1 lakh plan: a slow ETF core live, everything else on paper

### Ranked: what to run, what to defer, what to avoid

The ranking below uses four tests: whether the strategy fits in ₹1 lakh, what it costs to run each year, what the after-cost evidence says, and how it behaves when the Nifty falls.

| Rank | Strategy | What to do at ₹1 lakh | Why it ranks here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Multi-asset ETF trend rotation (Nifty 50 ETF, gold ETF, liquid ETF) | Run live as the core, about ₹75,000 | Works in any size, cheap at monthly frequency, can sit in gold or cash when the Nifty falls; backtests 16–19% a year with ~23% worst falls, unaudited |
| 2 | Liquid ETF or arbitrage fund | Hold the rest (about ₹25,000) as a buffer and as the core's cash position | ~6.5–7% a year with no skill needed, which already beats the roughly 88% of F&O traders who lose |
| 3 | Hedged positional option selling (iron condor or iron fly, held several days, volatility-filtered) | Paper-trade now; go live at ₹2–5 lakh | The only option strategy with an economic reason to win (an insurance premium, earned mostly overnight) and the closest to direction-neutral; at ₹1 lakh one lot eats 40–80% of capital and cannot be staggered |
| 4 | Intraday ORB-style option buying | Paper-trade now; go live only when one lot's stop is ≤1% of capital (about ₹2.6 lakh) | The only buying system with a multi-year positive after-cost test, but ~48% win rate, fading since mid-2024, and needs the ₹500 data feed |
| 5 | Two-way MCX trend following on daily prices | Paper-trade now; go live near ₹5 lakh | Can profit from falling prices, but evidence is weak, crude was the main drag, and one lot is too coarse to size at 1% risk |
| Avoid | Unhedged straddles and strangles; Nifty, Bank Nifty and Sensex futures; hero-zero; 9:20 straddle variants; high-frequency intraday equity; do-it-yourself arbitrage; pairs; market-neutral | Do not run | Margin exceeds capital, or results are negative after costs |

Hedged selling ranks above ORB buying even though both are deferred. Its edge has an economic explanation backed by peer-reviewed research, and it comes closest to the direction-neutral profile you asked for. ORB's edge rests on a single broker newsletter's backtest and has been fading. The capital thresholds come from simple sizing arithmetic. One ORB lot's ₹2,600 stop is 1% of capital at about ₹2.6 lakh. A ₹5,000 Crude Mini stop is 1% at ₹5 lakh. And practitioners suggest ₹2–5 lakh to run hedged selling across several staggered entries so that no single bad day dominates.

The "avoid" row has two kinds of reasons. Unhedged option selling and index futures need ₹1.1–3.25 lakh of margin per lot, more than the account. Hero-zero and the recent 9:20 straddle variants lost money after costs. Do-it-yourself arbitrage, pairs and market-neutral books need positions worth lakhs per leg. High-frequency intraday equity is where SEBI finds the highest loss rates. If you insist on trading options live now, the least-bad version is one lot of ORB-style buying on liquid strikes near the current price, at most one trade a day, a hard 20% stop, and a fixed ₹10,000 experiment budget that ends the experiment once it is gone. Treat that money as tuition. At about 2.6% risk per trade, an ordinary losing streak can use it up before the results mean anything.

### How to split the ₹1 lakh

| Sleeve | Amount | Instruments | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core rotation (live) | ₹75,000 | Nifty 50 ETF, gold ETF, liquid ETF | Monthly; hold the stronger asset if it beats cash, otherwise the liquid ETF |
| Buffer | ₹25,000 | Liquid ETF or arbitrage fund | Covers fees and drawdowns; never used as margin |
| Satellites | ₹0 live | Paper trading only | Hedged selling, ORB buying, MCX trend, until the capital thresholds above |

Start from the tested template rather than inventing a new one. Each month, compare the trailing return of a Nifty 50 ETF and a gold ETF with a liquid ETF. Use one lookback of 3, 6 or 12 months, chosen once, before you test anything. Hold the stronger asset if it beats cash; otherwise hold the liquid ETF. Add silver or a second holding only if it passes the same out-of-sample test, because none of the backtests found for this report included silver. At stock-delivery rates, a full ₹75,000 switch costs about ₹180, so even monthly switching costs about 2% a year. ETF charges may be lower, so confirm on Zerodha's calculator. Each switch also triggers tax on any gains, and none of the published backtests modelled that. Because the signals use daily closing prices, the bot can run on the free Personal API tier with end-of-day prices from a free source, which avoids the ₹500 data fee. Only order placement needs the static IP.

### Step-by-step build

| Step | When | What to do | Move on only when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Park the capital | Week 1 | Move the full ₹1 lakh into a liquid ETF or arbitrage fund so it earns while you build | No gate |

### Staying inside SEBI's algo rules on Kite

SEBI's retail algo circular of 4 February 2025 applies in full to all brokers from **1 April 2026**, after two deferrals ([SEBI](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/feb-2025/safer-participation-of-retail-investors-in-algorithmic-trading_91614.html); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/sebi-extends-retail-algo-trading-framework-rollout-to-2026-125093000956_1.html)). It bans open APIs. Access now needs a broker-issued API key, OAuth login, two-factor authentication and **a static IP whitelisted by the broker** ([IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/civil/sebi-retail-investors-algorithmic-trading/)). Zerodha's rule is that from 1 April 2026 you must have a static IP to place orders through the API. Market-data streaming and read-only endpoints such as order book and positions still work from any IP. You register the IP under "IP Whitelist" in your Kite Connect profile, with one primary IP, one optional secondary, and **one change allowed per calendar week**. The IP can come from an internet provider, a cloud host or a VPN, and IPv6 is allowed. Rotating or shared proxy IPs are unsuitable, and sharing an IP is allowed only with immediate family ([Zerodha static IP](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/general-kite/kite-api/articles/static-ip); [Zerodha FAQ](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/general-kite/kite-api/articles/kite-connect-api-faqs)). In practice, a cloud setup whose outbound IP changes, or a home connection with a dynamic IP, will have its orders rejected, so run the bot on a fixed host.

A bot you build yourself does **not need exchange registration if it stays at or below 10 orders per second**. Kite enforces that limit per client account and rejects anything faster ([Kite forum](https://kite.trade/forum/discussion/15912/preparing-to-comply-with-sebis-retail-algo-rules-static-ip-ratelimits-order-types)). The bot may be used only by you and your family, meaning spouse, dependent children and dependent parents ([SEBI](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/feb-2025/safer-participation-of-retail-investors-in-algorithmic-trading_91614.html); [IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/civil/sebi-retail-investors-algorithmic-trading/)). Selling or sharing it beyond family brings in exchange registration and empanelment, and providers of "black-box" algos (logic not disclosed) must register as SEBI Research Analysts. Since 1 April 2026, market orders must carry **market protection** (a price limit on how far the order can fill from the current price), and orders with protection set to zero are rejected ([Kite forum](https://kite.trade/forum/discussion/comment/52171)). The forum also reports caps of 400 orders a minute and 5,000 a day per API key ([Kite forum](https://kite.trade/forum/discussion/comment/52868/)), but these do not appear on the official documentation page. Some blogs say algo servers must be hosted in India ([Sahi](https://www.sahi.com/blogs/sebi-algo-trading-rules-2026-what-every-retail-trader-must-know-before-april)). That is unconfirmed, so ask Zerodha before choosing a host.

Be wary of anyone selling "2% a day". In 2022 SEBI barred brokers from citing the past or expected returns of any algo ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/markets/sebi-issues-guidelines-for-brokers-providing-algorithmic-trading-services-122090201262_1.html)). Since 2025, return claims must be verified by a SEBI-recognised Past Risk and Return Verification Agency (PaRRVA) ([Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/04/223-sebi-parrva-guidelines-updates-algo-trading-rules/)). In October 2026 SEBI's final order against Avadhut Sathe Trading Academy directed refunds of ₹546.17 crore plus ₹67.87 crore in interest and imposed ₹11.5 crore in penalties. Among SEBI's findings: the academy publicised profitable trades while participants lost money overall ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/sebi-orders-avadhut-sathe-trading-academy-and-its-founder-to-refund-546-cr-126102301645_1.html)).

### Risk limits to hard-code before the first live order

An algo removes hesitation, revenge trading and moved stops, but only for the rules you actually write into it. It does nothing about overfitting, bad cost assumptions, changing market conditions or edges that fade. So the controls have to be in the code before any money is live, and the backtest discipline has to come before the code.

| Control | Setting on ₹1 lakh | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Risk per trade | ₹250–1,000 (0.25–1% of equity) | ¼ to ½ Kelly on an edge you have already halved |
| Daily loss stop | ₹2,000–3,000 (2–3%): close everything and stop for the day | Stops one bad day turning into a blown account |
| Weekly loss stop | ₹5,000 (5%): stop until you review it manually | This report's suggestion |
| Account circuit breaker | Halt all trading if equity falls below ₹80,000 | This report's suggestion; a 20% loss needs a 25% gain to recover |
| Strategy retirement | Live drawdown beyond 1.5–2× the worst backtest drawdown | A sign the edge has decayed or never existed |
| Leverage | No unhedged short options, no futures, no intraday (MIS) leverage | Margin and tail risk exceed the account |
| Order hygiene | Limit orders or market protection; a maximum quantity per order; stay well below 10 orders per second | Kite rejects unprotected market orders; bursts above 10 per second are blocked |
| Failure handling | A heartbeat check that freezes or closes positions on disconnect; a manual kill switch; alerts | Protects against API outages and runaway loops |
| Backtest discipline | Log every variant; no more than about 45 variants per 5 years of daily data; walk-forward plus one untouched holdout; halve backtest returns before sizing | Overfitting research ([Bailey et al.](https://www.ams.org/notices/201405/rnoti-p458.pdf); [Bailey summary](https://sdm.lbl.gov/oapapers/ssrn-id2507040-bailey.pdf)); post-publication decay ([McLean & Pontiff](https://www.gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf)) |

### Tax shapes which strategies make sense

Profits from F&O on a recognised exchange count as non-speculative business income, and profits from intraday equity count as speculative business income. Both are taxed at your slab rate and reported on ITR-3. F&O losses can be carried forward eight years and speculative losses four, provided you file the return on time ([Groww](https://groww.in/blog/how-traders-are-taxed-in-india); [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/fo-trader-return-filing)). The Income-tax Act 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026, so the relevant sections have new numbers ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1056974)). Delivery trades are generally taxed as capital gains instead ([Groww](https://groww.in/blog/how-traders-are-taxed-in-india)). Arbitrage funds are taxed like equity: 20% on gains held under a year, and 12.5% on longer-term gains above ₹1.25 lakh a year ([arthgyaan](https://arthgyaan.com/fund/tata-arbitrage-fund-direct-growth-option/145724)). Check the treatment of gold and silver ETFs separately. Keeping the bot to delivery-based ETF trades avoids the speculative-income category entirely. If you ever declare F&O losses, ask a chartered accountant whether a tax audit applies.

## Conclusion

The binding limit at ₹1 lakh is not a lack of clever strategies. It is that fixed costs, contract sizes and per-order brokerage are built for accounts ten or more times larger. A ₹500 data plan plus a static IP costs a ₹1 lakh trader 7–10% a year before the first trade, while a ₹20 lakh account pays well under 1% for the same setup. A one-lot option trader pays more brokerage per unit than a five-lot trader. And a ₹15 lakh index lot makes any futures position 15 times leverage. That is why the evidence points to spending the small account on the one strategy whose costs shrink with the account, a slow ETF rotation. The bot's job at this size is to enforce discipline and build a verifiable live record, not to generate income.

The biggest risk in the plan as first framed is the target itself. A daily rupee goal quietly sets the bet size, and the bet size that 2% a day demands destroys accounts even when the edge is real. The route to ₹2,000 a day runs through capital. At a strong 15–25% a year it needs ₹20–33 lakh. As an illustration, ₹1 lakh plus ₹20,000 a month of savings, compounding at 15%, would pass ₹20 lakh in about six years. A bot that is still running at that point, with a clean live record, is worth far more than one that chased 2% a day and joined the 88% who lose.
